Pricing your Coffee
Understand how the price of your coffee is actually built — from the market reference and differentials through to the costs you carry — so you negotiate from knowledge rather than guesswork.
Pricing your Coffee
This session walks through how a green coffee price is put together, what a differential reflects, and how to work out your own cost of production so you know your floor before a conversation starts.
Before you mark this complete, put numbers to your own farm: your cost per kilogram last harvest, and the price you would need to cover it with a margin worth farming for. Bring those two figures to every buyer conversation.

International Coffee Logistics
Getting coffee from your mill to a roaster's warehouse runs through exporters, shipping lines, customs and a lot of paperwork. Knowing the steps helps you set realistic timelines and avoid expensive surprises.
International Coffee Logistics
Follow a container from origin to destination — milling and preparation, sampling and approval, export documentation, freight, and final delivery — and see where delays and unexpected costs usually appear.
Note which stages you handle yourself and which depend on an exporter or partner. The handoffs are where most problems start, so agree in writing who is responsible for what, and by when.

Considerate Communication Tips
Most deals are lost to silence rather than disagreement. A few habits in how and when you reply can make you considerably easier to buy from.
Considerate Communication Tips
This covers the practical habits: replying within a predictable window, writing clearly across a language barrier, raising bad news early rather than late, and keeping a record of what was agreed.
Pick one habit to adopt this season — a standing reply time, or a short update at every shipment milestone. Consistency will do more for you than polish.

Forming Equitable Commercial Relationships
A good buyer relationship outlasts any single harvest. This task looks at what makes an agreement fair on both sides, and what makes it durable across seasons.
Forming Equitable Commercial Relationships
Explore what is worth agreeing up front — volume, quality expectations, how price is set, payment terms and timing — and how a longer commitment changes what both sides are able to plan for.
Think about your current buyers: which agreements are genuinely clear, and which rest on assumptions neither side has said out loud? Write down the terms you want confirmed before next harvest.

Perceptions & Definition of Quality & Value
Quality means different things to a farmer, an exporter and a roaster. Understanding how buyers judge and value your coffee helps you present it on terms they already recognise.
Perceptions & Definition of Quality & Value
This session looks at how cup scores, defect counts, processing and year-to-year consistency feed into a buyer's decision — and where value comes from things that never appear on a score sheet.
Consider what you already do that a buyer would value but has no way of knowing about. Those details belong in your profile and your story; they rarely come through in a sample alone.
